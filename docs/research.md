# 🌿 GrinBot Garden — Research & Inspiration

## Concept: Digital Gardens & Passive Simulations

### What is a Digital Garden?
A digital garden is a personal website that's less "finished blog post" and more "living notebook" — things grow, change, and evolve over time. For our purposes, we're taking this literally: a visual, interactive space that evolves on its own.

---

## Cool Aesthetic Directions

### 1. **The Mycelium Network** 🍄
- Underground fungal networks that pulse and grow
- Nodes represent thoughts/ideas, connected by glowing hyphae
- Particles travel along connections like nutrients
- Dark, bioluminescent aesthetic (think Avatar's forest at night)

**Why it's cool:**
- Fungi are having a moment culturally
- Perfect metaphor for interconnected thoughts
- Can visualize real data (Moltbook ponders) as "spore clouds"

**Visual style:** Deep purples, electric blues, glowing cyan nodes

---

### 2. **Orbital Biomes (Little Planets)** 🪐
- Tiny spherical worlds floating in space
- Each planet has its own ecosystem
- They slowly rotate, seasons change
- Occasional visitors (meteors, comets, ships)

**Why it's cool:**
- Very satisfying to watch
- Each planet could represent a different theme/day
- Can add new planets over time
- Great for mobile (one planet = one view)

**Visual style:** Stylized low-poly, pastel colors, soft lighting

---

### 3. **The Data Coral Reef** 🪸
- Underwater scene with coral that grows based on activity
- Fish schools = visitors or interactions
- Coral formations shaped by data patterns
- Day/night cycle with different creatures

**Why it's cool:**
- Coral is beautiful and procedurally generatable
- Can reflect "health" of different data sources
- Very calming to watch
- Could have rare "events" (bioluminescent blooms)

**Visual style:** Caustic lighting, translucent materials, particle bubbles

---

### 4. **Zen Garden + Code** 🏔️
- Traditional Japanese rock garden meets generative art
- Raked sand patterns that evolve algorithmically
- Stones that represent projects/milestones
- Occasional ripples from "events"

**Why it's cool:**
- Minimal, calming aesthetic
- Perfect for "pondering" visualization
- Patterns can represent thought processes
- Very GitHub Pages friendly (simple geometry)

**Visual style:** Minimal, monochrome with accent colors, clean lines

---

### 5. **The Library of Babel (Infinite Bookshelves)** 📚
- Endless library with books that glow when topics are "hot"
- Floating islands connected by bridges
- Books open to reveal quotes/insights
- Dust particles, light shafts through windows

**Why it's cool:**
- Perfect for knowledge/research themes
- Can showcase Moltbook ponders as "found books"
- Very atmospheric
- Scales beautifully (can always add more shelves)

**Visual style:** Warm tones, volumetric lighting, paper textures

## Technical Considerations

### Why Three.js + GitHub Pages?
- **Free hosting** — perfect for a side project
- **No backend needed** — all client-side
- **Can fetch APIs** — Moltbook, weather, etc.
- **Easy to iterate** — just push to deploy

### Performance Targets
- 60fps on mobile
- <5MB initial load
- Progressive enhancement (works without WebGL)

### Procedural Generation
All these concepts rely on procedural generation so they can:
- Run infinitely without repeating
- Respond to data inputs
- Stay lightweight (no huge asset files)
